01 · The brief

Deals, pantry and recipes in one flow

Supermarkets discount items daily before they'd otherwise be thrown out. The hard part isn't finding a single deal, it's getting all the way from "here are some cheap groceries" to "here's a meal I can actually cook tonight" without manually tracking prices, distance, and what's already in the fridge.

The app ties the three steps together: find deals near you based on how you get around (walking, cycling, driving), match them automatically against your pantry, and get concrete recipes with the savings worked out, the missing ingredients listed, and a shopping list ready to hand off to Nemlig or Wolt.

02 · How it fits together

From Salling deals to recipe suggestions

The data flow splits in two: a daily background job that collects deals into a cache, and the actual search, which combines the cache with the user's pantry and an AI model.

Salling APIdiscounted items per store
→
Daily cron jobVercel, 7am
→
Supabase cache6 postal codes
→
Searchaddress (DAWA) + mode of transport
→
Claude Haikumatch against pantry → recipes

Claude Haiku is the primary recipe engine and receives current deals plus the pantry as context; it prioritises items expiring soon. If it doesn't respond, the app silently falls back to Spoonacular, then TheMealDB, and finally a local, hand-written recipe dataset, so the "Cook" tab is never empty.

06 · Limits and deliberate omissions

What the app deliberately isn't

No automated tests

Unlike other projects, no test suite has been built yet. Each change is instead verified manually against a production build and with screenshots of the running app.

Copenhagen only

Data collection covers six postal codes around Copenhagen. Adding more is a configuration change, not a code change, but it hasn't been done yet.

No real user accounts

Profile, pantry and stats live in the browser's localStorage. That's enough for personal use, but data doesn't follow you across devices.

No operational numbers yet

Unlike the news monitor, the app has no dedicated user generating daily logs. The figures here are snapshots from the live cache, not a week-long audit.
